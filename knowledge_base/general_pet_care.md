# General Pet Care Guide

## Establishing Consistent Routines

Consistent daily routines reduce anxiety and promote healthy behavior in pets. Feed, walk, and interact with your pets at the same times each day. Predictability helps pets feel secure and makes it easier for owners to track health changes.

A sample daily schedule framework for multi-pet households:
- 07:00 — Morning feeding (all pets)
- 07:30 — Dog walk / cat playtime
- 12:00 — Midday check-in / litter scoop
- 17:00–18:00 — Evening feeding (all pets)
- 19:00 — Evening play / enrichment
- 20:00 — Medication administration if needed

## Scheduling Multiple Pets

When caring for multiple pets, avoid assigning tasks at identical times to prevent neglecting one pet while caring for another. Stagger feeding times by 15–30 minutes if pets compete for attention or have separate feeding areas.

Keep species-specific tasks separate: dogs and cats have different dietary, exercise, and enrichment needs. A shared schedule tool should track each pet individually while providing an aggregated daily view.

## Veterinary Care Schedule

| Life Stage | Vet Visit Frequency |
|---|---|
| Puppy/Kitten (0–1 yr) | Monthly for first 4 months, then every 3–4 months |
| Adult (1–7 yrs) | Annual wellness exam |
| Senior (7+ yrs) | Bi-annual exam with bloodwork |

Keep vaccination records, microchip numbers, and medication histories in an accessible location. Consider a dedicated pet health notebook or digital record.

## Emergency Preparedness

Signs requiring immediate veterinary care:
- Difficulty breathing or labored breathing
- Seizures or loss of consciousness
- Suspected ingestion of toxins (chocolate, xylitol, grapes, lilies)
- Inability to urinate (especially cats — this is life-threatening)
- Profuse bleeding that doesn't stop with gentle pressure
- Sudden paralysis or inability to stand
- Pale, blue, or white gums

Keep your veterinarian's number and the nearest 24-hour emergency animal hospital number saved in your phone.

## Medication Management

If your pet is on medication:
- Administer at the same time each day for best efficacy.
- Use a pill pocket, soft treat, or pill gun to ensure the full dose is consumed.
- Never stop medications abruptly without veterinary guidance.
- Store medications as directed (room temperature vs. refrigerated).
- Log each dose to confirm administration (especially in multi-person households).

## Environmental Safety

Common household hazards for pets:
- **Toxic foods**: chocolate, grapes, raisins, xylitol (sugar substitute), onions, garlic, macadamia nuts.
- **Toxic plants**: lilies (lethal to cats), azaleas, oleander, sago palm.
- **Household chemicals**: cleaning products, essential oil diffusers (especially harmful to cats), rodenticide.
- **Physical hazards**: loose electrical cords, small swallowable objects, unsecured trash cans.

## Sleep and Rest

Adult pets need significant rest time. Dogs sleep 12–14 hours daily; cats 12–16 hours. Provide dedicated, comfortable rest areas away from high-traffic zones. Do not disturb pets during sleep — they can be startled and react defensively.

## Social and Emotional Wellbeing

Pets thrive with regular positive interaction. Beyond feeding and physical care, schedule at least 15–30 minutes of dedicated quality time daily — play, grooming, training, or simply sitting together. This strengthens the human-animal bond and helps you notice subtle changes in behavior that may indicate health issues.
