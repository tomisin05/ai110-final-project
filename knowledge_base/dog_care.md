# Dog Care Guide

## Feeding Guidelines

Adult dogs should be fed twice daily — once in the morning (around 07:00) and once in the evening (around 17:00–18:00). Puppies under 6 months need three meals per day. Avoid feeding large meals immediately before or after vigorous exercise, which can cause bloat, especially in large breeds.

Always provide fresh, clean water throughout the day. Change water at least once daily to prevent bacterial growth. Dogs typically need about 1 ounce of water per pound of body weight per day.

## Exercise Requirements

Dogs require daily physical activity to maintain healthy weight and mental well-being. Exercise needs vary by breed and age:

- Small breeds (Chihuahua, Pomeranian): 20–30 minutes of moderate exercise daily.
- Medium breeds (Beagle, Cocker Spaniel): 30–60 minutes daily.
- Large/active breeds (Labrador, German Shepherd, Border Collie): 60–90 minutes or more daily, ideally split across two walks.
- Senior dogs: shorter, gentler walks 2–3 times per day.

Morning walks between 07:00–09:00 are ideal: cooler temperature, lower traffic, and good mental stimulation before the day begins.

## Grooming Schedule

Regular grooming prevents matting, skin infections, and keeps shedding manageable.

- Brushing: short-coat breeds weekly; long-coat breeds 3–4 times per week.
- Bathing: every 4–6 weeks using dog-safe shampoo. Over-bathing strips natural oils.
- Nail trimming: every 3–4 weeks, or when you hear clicking on hard floors.
- Ear cleaning: check weekly; clean monthly or when visibly dirty. Floppy-eared breeds are prone to infections.
- Teeth brushing: ideally daily; minimum 3 times per week to prevent dental disease.

## Health Monitoring

Perform a weekly at-home health check:

- Run hands along ribs — you should feel them but not see them (healthy weight).
- Check eyes for discharge or cloudiness.
- Check ears for odor, redness, or excessive wax.
- Inspect paws and nails for cracks or overgrowth.
- Monitor stool consistency — firm stools indicate good digestive health.

Annual veterinary exams are recommended for adult dogs; semi-annual for seniors (7+ years). Keep vaccinations, flea/tick, and heartworm prevention up to date.

## Training and Mental Enrichment

Dogs need mental stimulation in addition to physical exercise. Schedule short training sessions (5–10 minutes, 2–3 times per day) to reinforce commands and prevent boredom-driven destructive behavior. Puzzle toys, sniff walks, and socialization with other dogs also provide excellent enrichment.

## Common Health Concerns

- **Obesity**: The most common preventable health problem in dogs. Monitor weight monthly.
- **Dental disease**: Affects 80% of dogs by age 3. Daily brushing is the most effective prevention.
- **Joint problems**: Large breeds are prone to hip dysplasia. Avoid high-impact exercise on hard surfaces for young dogs whose growth plates are still forming.
- **Separation anxiety**: Gradually desensitize dogs to departures; establish consistent routines.
